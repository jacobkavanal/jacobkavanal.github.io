Liver risk stratification

Engineered longitudinal clinical features and combined survival models for the ANNITIA challenge. The submitted ensemble achieved a 0.93473 private-leaderboard C-index.

Python · NumPy · scikit-learn · Survival analysis

Feature engineering & model experimentation · June 2026

Overview

Clinical follow-up is uneven. Patients have different numbers of visits, biomarkers are missing, and some patients leave observation before an outcome occurs. A useful model needs to account for trajectories and censoring rather than treating every row as a complete snapshot.

My contribution

For the challenge, I engineered patient-level trajectory features and worked with endpoint-specific survival models. The résumé reports 255 features for 1,676 patients with up to 22 visits, including FIB-4, APRI, biomarker trends, and missingness indicators.

Model choice

The submitted approach rank-blended Coxnet, Random Survival Forest, and XGBoost Cox predictions. These model families capture different relationships: regularized linear effects, nonlinear splits, and boosted risk functions. Rank blending combines relative ordering without assuming their raw prediction scales are comparable.

Validation

Project notes distinguish the official submission from a separate local model-family search. The best local cross-validation family was not the submitted ensemble. Sparse hepatic events made fold-level comparisons noisy, so local CV and private-leaderboard scores should not be presented as directly comparable estimates.

Evidence & results

¹ The résumé reports a private-leaderboard C-index of 0.93473 and 27th place in the official challenge. A C-index measures concordance in risk ordering; it is not classification accuracy or proof of calibrated individual risk. The exact score and placement are résumé-reported and have not been independently verified here against the leaderboard.

Limitations & next steps

This is a competition project, not a clinically validated decision system. Further work should examine target construction, missing-label handling, censoring assumptions, calibration, subgroup performance, and external validation. The repository notes include later target-correctness fixes, which must be kept separate from the scored submission.

Sources: résumé and project documentation, November 2026. Proposed next steps are separate from completed work.
